# Three chronology debates against the drift data; the "purāṇa" reference sweep

2026-08-14. Three questions raised against the sweet-spot maps (W1-500 /
C3-500, manifest `dicsep2026_n127_ppl`), with a reference sweep over
`corpus/epic_puranas/` for what early authors meant by "purāṇa".

## 1. Does the Rāmāyaṇa trail the Mahābhārata?

Feature-system split, not a uniform trail:

- **C3 (sandhied trigrams): yes.** All seven kāṇḍas sit at percentile 10–17,
  above the whole MBh core (0–7, medians: MBh 7 vs Rām 14) though below the
  didactic Śānti/Anuśāsana (25–26).
- **W1 (function words): no.** Medians 10 (MBh) vs 9 (Rām); Bāla at 1,
  Uttara at 4.

Reading: the Rām's phonological/morphological texture is younger — more
classically regularized, consistent with its ādikāvya polish and more
unitary redaction — while its function-word habits are as archaic as the
MBh's. "Composition slightly postdates the MBh core" is defensible on C3
only, and within-epic microstructure is near the method's resolution
(the tiny closing parvans sit at 0–2). Two flags: (a) the kāṇḍas are
internally compressed with no Bāla/Uttara lateness signature — against the
received view of those books as late additions; (b) the reference sweep
below shows the Rām (late books included) has zero genre-awareness of
"purāṇa" (its 11 hits are gopurāṇi "gates" and purāṇa- "ancient"), while the
MBh frame layers use the full apparatus — a different textual culture, not
simply a later date.

## 2. Bhāgavata: archaizing artifact or genuinely early?

**[ANNOTATION 2026-08-14 (Kengo): the verdict below is a session hypothesis,
not my position.** The three measurements stand as data — the MFW
scale-dependence (19 @30 → 52 @80 → 26 @5000), the epic-marker
over-performance (39%), and the split profile. What I do not endorse is the
inferential leap from them to "artifact / imitation": that the BhP's
placement is *explained* by deliberate archaizing, and that its composition
date is thereby settled as late. The U-shape shows the BhP's position is
scale-sensitive in a way the epics' is not; whether the cause is imitation,
an unusual register interacting differently with different feature strata,
or partly genuine antiquity in the particle layer, remains open. The closing
sentence's "external anchors" claim is also wrong as stated: the reuse
analysis found (a) that other purāṇas reused the *ViP's* text — a fact about
the ViP's circulation that implies nothing about the BhP's date — and
(b) that the BhP shares almost zero text with anything else in the corpus,
the ViP included. There is no shared text on which a BhP→ViP dependency
argument could stand; "ViP dependency" must not be cited as an anchor for
BhP lateness. Until this is resolved, article prose must not cite this
section's "artifact" conclusion as established; the BhP's date remains one
of the open questions the drift analysis poses, not one it has answered.]**

Artifact, on three convergent diagnostics:

- **Scale dependence (the U-shape).** Mean W1 drift percentile of the BhP
  skandhas by MFW: 19 @30, 52 @80, 50 @500, 47 @1500, 26 @5000. Genuinely
  early texts stay early at every scale (MBh 25/12/11/11; Rām 7/7/9/11).
  The BhP is "early" precisely where the most salient, most imitable
  features live — the top particles and the open-class lexicon — and
  mid-corpus across the broad unconscious habit profile.
- **Over-performance.** On the 120 words most discriminative between the
  epic and late blocks, the BhP sides with the epics 39% of the time — more
  than the genuinely old purāṇic core does (Mārk-1-80 32%, ViP 28%,
  Matsya 26%; epics 91–98%, late block 5%). A text younger than the old
  core that outdoes it on epic markers is imitating.
- **Hybrid profile.** Where it sides epic: particles (sma, iva) and absence
  of late ritual-manual vocabulary. Where it sides late: the entire epic
  narrative register (rājan, abravīt, bhārata, battle terms). No genuinely
  early text shows this split.

Stylometry cannot date a skilled archaizer from position alone; the
scale-dependence diagnostic is the tell, and the external anchors (ViP
dependency, late quotation history) stand. Its mid placement at the sweet
spot is the *average* of imitated-early and residual-late signal.

## 3. Mārkaṇḍeya, and what "the purāṇa" meant

Drift data: **[CORRECTED 2026-08-14 evening — see
`2026-08-14_sp_mark_ppl_sequence.md`.** The original sentence here
misattributed a CI and the "earliest non-PPL" title: Mārk 1–80 actually
sits at W1 45 [40,48] / C3 60 — the *latest* Mārkaṇḍeya layer. The
earliest-styled non-PPL purāṇic units are the old Skandapurāṇa whole
(W1 26 [24,26] / C3 40 [40,43]) and Mārkaṇḍeya 94–141, the
pañcalakṣaṇa-content block (W1 27 [23,28] / C3 31 [27,34]).] Mārk still
moves *earlier* when reuse is removed (its own voice, not borrowed
material), and the PPL Textgruppen I/II at 22–38 remain a corpus
reconstruction, not a transmitted title.

Reference sweep (with false positives weeded: Rām gopurāṇi; MBh 14's
"satyaṃ... pañcalakṣaṇam" = five marks of truth; V1.44 ≈ Mārk 49.11
"śabdādi-viṣayaḥ... pañcalakṣaṇaḥ" = five sense qualities, a shared PPL
verse):

- **Epic-zone usage is singular and genealogical.** MBh 3: "vāyuproktam
  anusmṛtya purāṇam ṛṣisaṃstutam" — *the* purāṇa, Vāyu-proclaimed. HV cites
  "purāṇe" (loc. sing.) for sons-and-grandsons content: "purāṇe kīrtitās
  tāta putrāḥ pautrāś ca". MBh 1: itihāsapurāṇa as Veda-supplement.
  Rām: nothing.
- **The genre self-definition is late-stratum.** The pañcalakṣaṇa verse
  ("sargaś ca pratisargaś ca... purāṇaṃ pañcalakṣaṇam") occurs in PPL
  **Textgruppe IIB** — Kirfel's *late* stratum (drift 64–70), not I/II —
  and thence Bḍ 1,1.37, Matsya 53.65, Mārk 134, ViP. The early PPL stratum
  does not yet name or define "purāṇa".
- **Plural/canon awareness is later still.** purāṇeṣu/purāṇāni and the
  aṣṭādaśa list concentrate in Matsya (its ch. 53 canon chapter; plur 17,
  aṣṭādaśa 16) and the late block; epic-zone texts are singular-dominant.
- Bḍ colophons style the whole work "brahmāṇḍe mahāpurāṇe vāyuprokte" —
  the Vāyu-prokta identity attached to the container.

**Synthesis**: what 4th–6th-century authors referred to was *the* purāṇa —
a single, Vāyu-attributed, genealogical corpus, i.e. the PPL/Vāyu–Brahmāṇḍa
ancestor complex; the drift axis puts exactly that stratum earliest. "Purāṇa"
as a genre of many titled texts (definition verse, 18-canon) is a
later-stratum development. So: the PPL material held the name before any
surviving purāṇa did, and the Mārkaṇḍeya (1–80) is the earliest surviving
text *composed as* a purāṇa — both true, at different levels of the word.

Caveat: colophon inflation — chapter colophons ("iti śrī...purāṇe...")
dominate raw singular counts inside purāṇas; the usage analysis above rests
on non-colophon citation contexts in the epic-zone texts.
