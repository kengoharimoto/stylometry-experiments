# BhP and the Brahmasūtra: the inbound dependence channel

2026-08-18. Side-product of an E-texts session on Brahmasūtra (BS)
references *outside* its own commentarial/Vedāntic traditions (full
report, scan scripts, and hit tables:
`/mnt2/kengo/E-texts/40_tools/reports/brahmasutra_external_refs/`).
Kengo asked what the BhP results mean for the BhP's provenance. This
note ports them into the chronology project, because they are the
mirror image of the three 08-17 sweeps: those asked *who quotes the
BhP* (outbound — answer: nobody datable before ~1000); this asks *what
the BhP itself presupposes* (inbound). Method: all 545 sūtras
(GRETIL `badarayana_brahmasutra_c.txt`, "BBs" numbering ≠ vulgate by
1–2 in places) run as (a) normalized substring quote-scan over the
whole 1-GB library and (b) stem-overlap scan against all 14,032 BhP
verses (`bhp_echoes.txt` in the report dir); plus targeted greps.

## The BhP's BS-dependent material, by stratum

| locus | text | assessment |
|---|---|---|
| BhP 1.1.1 | *janmādy asya yato 'nvayād itarataś ca…* | BS 1.1.2 verbatim as opening unit, immediately glossed (*anvayād itarataś ca* = anvaya-vyatireka, as the school reads it) — quote-and-gloss behavior |
| BhP 1.1.7 + 42× | *bhagavān bādarāyaṇaḥ*; Śuka = *bādarāyaṇiḥ* throughout | self-attribution to the BS's traditional author |
| BhP 1.5.4 (frame) | *jijñāsitam adhītaṃ ca brahma yat tat sanātanam* — Nārada to the dejected Vyāsa | the frame presupposes a **completed brahmajijñāsā** that leaves Vyāsa unsatisfied; the BhP is commissioned as its completion |
| BhP 2.9.35 (catuḥślokī) | *etāvad eva jijñāsyaṃ tattva-jijñāsunā … anvaya-vyatirekābhyāṃ yat syāt sarvatra sarvadā* | jijñāsā + method vocabulary |
| BhP 3.26.6; 5.14.10 | *parābhidhyānena kartṛtvaṃ prakṛteḥ pumān … manyate*; *parābhidhyānena vibhraṃśita-smṛtiḥ* | **the anchor.** In the BS-technical sense (jīva's mis-identification; BS 3.2.5 *parābhidhyānāt tu tirohitam*) the word occurs NOWHERE else in the 1-GB library outside the BS + its commentaries (AgP hit = abhicāra sense; Tattvasaṃgraha hit = para/apara cognition). Sits in the *body* (Kapilagītā; Bharata story), not the frame |
| BhP 3.26.49; 7.7.22; 7.15.54; 11.7.42; 11.28.20 | *…samanvayāt* / *samanvayena vyatirekataś ca* (5× samanvay-) | BS 1.1.4's method-word, shifted from exegetical to ontological sense (what persists through states is real) |
| BhP 12.13.12+15 (colophon) | *sarva-vedānta-sāraṃ yad brahmātmaikatva-lakṣaṇam … sarva-vedānta-sāraṃ hi śrī-bhāgavatam iṣyate* | claims the essence of a Vedānta system it thereby presupposes |

Negatives worth recording: BhP has **no** *anāvṛtti* anywhere (the
traditional "ends on BS 4.4.22" claim has no verbal basis); its one
*brahmasūtra* (8.18.14) is Vāmana's sacred thread; the Garuḍapurāṇa
verse the Gauḍīyas cite for BhP-as-BS-commentary (*artho 'yaṃ
brahmasūtrāṇām…*) is **absent from the extant GarP e-text** (survives
only in Vedānta-tradition quotations).

## Direction: BS → BhP, not the reverse

1. *janmādy asya yataḥ* is not Upaniṣadic wording — it is the
   sūtra-style condensation of TaittU 3.1 (*yato vā imāni bhūtāni
   jāyante*); a shared śruti source explains *yataḥ*, not the
   five-word unit. GarP 1.1.12 even pads it metri causa (*janmādy asya
   yato bhavet*).
2. In the BS the sūtra is architecturally load-bearing (1.1.1 jijñāsā
   → 1.1.2 lakṣaṇa → 1.1.3 śāstrayonitvāt → 1.1.4 samanvayāt); in BhP
   1.1.1 the same unit sits inside an ornate maṅgala and gets glossed
   and expanded — the quoting text's behavior.
3. The dependence is a *cluster* (opening + frame + parābhidhyāna +
   samanvaya + colophon), spread across skandhas 1, 2, 3, 5, 7, 11,
   12 — systemic, not one floating phrase.
4. The BS is presupposed by the whole commentarial record from Śaṅkara
   on; no accepted BhP date precedes it.

## Why this bears on the Q2 debate (archaizing artifact vs genuinely early)

Cf. `2026-08-14_three_debates_ram_bhp_markandeya.md` §2 and Kengo's
annotation there (the "artifact" verdict is a session hypothesis; the
ViP-dependency "anchor" was withdrawn).

- **A new external anchor, of a different kind.** The withdrawn anchor
  was outbound reuse; this one is inbound textual dependence: the BhP
  *quotes and presupposes the BS*. It is soft as a terminus post quem
  in itself (the BS is early — final redaction usually put ≤ c. 400–450
  CE), but hard as a claim about milieu: the author(s) worked inside
  śāstric Vedānta.
- **The base rate makes the milieu argument bite.** The E-texts survey
  found BS citation outside its own commentarial traditions to be rare
  and *late*: earliest datable outsiders are Vācaspati's
  Tattvavaiśāradī and Bhāsarvajña's Nyāyabhūṣaṇa (both 10th c.), the
  broad wave (Śaiva, Śākta/Śrīvidyā, Vīraśaiva, late Mīmāṃsā) later
  still. A non-commentarial text saturated with BS material centuries
  before *any* other non-commentarial text touches the BS would be an
  extreme outlier; in the 9th–10th c. it is unremarkable. Converges
  with the outbound silence (08-17 note: no BhP-distinctive quote in
  any datable author before ~1000) on the same window — the two
  independent channels now point the same way.
- **What it does to "genuinely early".** The BS-dependent material is
  not confined to a detachable frame: parābhidhyāna sits in the
  Kapilagītā (skandha 3) and the Bharata narrative (5), samanvaya in
  3, 7 and 11. A genuinely-early BhP now requires a heavy-redaction
  model in which opening, frame, colophon *and* these body passages
  are all secondary while the particle habits are original — at which
  point "early BhP" no longer names a text. The U-shape's
  scale-dependence finding is untouched as data, but the residual
  "partly genuine antiquity in the particle layer" reading loses its
  most natural carrier; a prestige-synthesis product (epic style
  imitated + Vedānta prestige claimed) fits both measurements at once.
- **What it does NOT do.** It does not date the BhP absolutely, and it
  does not by itself refute pre-10th-c. composition — the sociological
  argument is a base-rate argument, not a proof. Treat as one
  convergent line, not an anchor to hang the article on.

## Scholarship

van Buitenen 1966 ("On the Archaism of the Bhāgavata Purāṇa") already
read BhP 1.1.1 as deliberate BS allusion and drew the same
milieu-conclusion; Sheridan 1986 (advaitic theism), Hardy 1983
(provenance frame). New here, apparently untabulated before: the 1.5.4
frame reading as brahmajijñāsā-presupposing, the corpus-unique
parābhidhyāna link, the skandha-wide samanvaya spread, and the
negative results (no anāvṛtti; GarP verse absent). Reception side:
R.M. Gupta 2007; Gupta & Valpey 2013; Venkatkrishnan, *Love in the
Time of Scholarship*.

## Caveats

- BBs numbering ≠ vulgate (e.g. *phalam ata upapatteḥ* = vulgate
  3.2.38); the report quotes sūtra text so refs are recoverable.
- *samanvaya* alone could in principle be independent Vedāntic koine;
  the argument leans on parābhidhyāna + the cluster, not on any single
  item.
- All based on the GRETIL BhP e-text; critical-edition variants for
  the seven echo loci unchecked.
- Direction claims concern *wording*; doctrine may still descend from
  the shared Upaniṣadic stock on both sides.
