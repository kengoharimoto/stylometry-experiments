# First references to / quotes from the Rāmāyaṇa: library sweep

2026-08-17. Companion to the Bhāgavata quotation-history question (whose
first external quotes were early-second-millennium) and to
`2026-08-14_purana_witnesses_library_sweep.md`. Lexical sweep of
`/mnt2/kengo/E-texts` (1_sanskr + 2_pali + 2_prakrt + 7_inscriptions) on
rāmāyaṇ / vālmīk / "mā niṣāda"; false positives weeded (valmīka "anthill"
in Dharmakīrti PVSV and Mañjuśrīmūlakalpa ritual clay; editorial
footnotes). Datings cross-checked on the web where noted.

## Headline

**The Rāmāyaṇa's external attestation begins ~1st–2nd c. CE and is dense
by the 7th — roughly a millennium before the BhP's first quotes.** But
the *mode* of reference differs from the MBh's throughout: the Rām is
named, praised, performed, and used as a grammar-school example; it is
rarely quoted as an authority. Kengo's impression is right in refined
form — it is not that the Rām goes unmentioned (270 files mention
rāmāyaṇ vs 305 mahābhārat, library-wide, excluding the texts
themselves); it is that *proof-text quotation* is asymmetric.

## The witnesses, in rough chronological order

## The negatives (as informative as the positives)

Zero mentions in: **Patañjali's Mahābhāṣya, Manu, Yājñavalkya,
Arthaśāstra, Kāmasūtra, Nāṭyaśāstra mūla** (all its hits are
Abhinavabhāratī), and — striking given the ādikāvya topos — the entire
early ālaṃkāra line: **Bhāmaha, Daṇḍin's Kāvyādarśa, Vāmana, Udbhaṭa,
Rudraṭa**. The "first poet" motif lives in kāvya-praśasti (Aśvaghoṣa,
Kālidāsa, the Campā inscription) for ~700 years before poetics
internalizes it (Ānandavardhana). Mahāvaṃsa's
"rāmāyaṇabhāratādilokiyāsu kathāsu" is in the late continuation, and
disparaging ("worldly tales").

## Quantifying the MBh asymmetry

Citation-formula counts in the dharmanibandhas (locative "rāmāyaṇe" vs
"mahābhārate"): Lakṣmīdhara's Kṛtyakalpataru ~28 vs ~200 across kāṇḍas;
Hemādri ~7 vs ~100. **Ratio ≈ 1:7–1:10.** The MBh functions as smṛti
(quotable authority, "bhārataṃ pañcamo vedaḥ"); the Rām functions as
kāvya — named as model, performed, alluded to, but seldom quoted as
proof-text. Both are *named* at similar rates library-wide; only the
authority role differs. (Same split the purāṇa-witnesses sweep found:
citation habits are register facts, not popularity facts.)

## Upshot for the article

If the BhP question was "why are external quotes so late?", the Rām
control shows what an early, continuously attested text looks like:
named inside the MBh itself, praised by a datable kāvya author by the
2nd c. CE, a counter-tradition (Jain) by the 3rd–4th, curricular and
trans-regional (Cambodia, Campā) by the 7th. The citation-*rarity* Kengo
sensed is real but orthogonal to date — it tracks the kāvya/smṛti genre
divide, not attestation depth. Useful as symmetric framing when the
article touches the BhP quotation-history argument: absence of
*authority-quotation* is weak evidence of date (the Rām has little of it
while being early-attested); absence of *any* datable reference,
performance, or curriculum presence (the BhP case pre-1000) is the
stronger kind.

Caveats: Sanskrit-library sweep + web spot-checks, not a testimonia
monograph (Brockington, Righteous Rāma, ch. 2 is the standard survey to
cite); Chinese/Tibetan Buddhist material only via secondary sources; the
MBh "Vālmīki śloka" (7.118.48) is an attribution whose verse the
constituted Rām lacks — cite as reception evidence, not as a text quote.
