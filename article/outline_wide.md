# Outline C — Broad interdisciplinary venue

**Candidate venues:** Humanities & Social Sciences Communications (Nature
portfolio; humanities-friendly, generous SI); PNAS Nexus; Royal Society
Open Science; PLOS ONE. **Audience assumption:** scientists and general
scholars; knows neither Sanskrit nor stylometry; expects IMRaD-ish
structure, a figure-driven argument, and one crisp headline.
**Length budget:** 5,000–8,000 words main text + extensive SI.
**Register:** the headline is the *mechanism* — language drift as a
measurable retention clock — demonstrated on one of the world's largest
undated text traditions, with the text-historical results as the payoff.

**Working title idea:** "A retention clock orders the undated scriptures
of Hinduism: stylometric drift in two million words of epic and purāṇic
Sanskrit."

---

## 1. Introduction (≈900 w)
The dating problem: foundational texts of a major world religion,
composed and accreted over ~a millennium, transmitted without dates —
a century of philology yields islands of relative order and famous
controversies. The idea: many small usage habits drift together; if
habit-loss is quasi-irreversible (glottochronology's retention insight,
Dollo's asymmetry), the fraction of an original habit-inventory retained
is a clock. Preview: two independent feature systems, one ordering
(ρ 0.95); the ordering is reproduced by habit-loss alone (ρ 0.94);
validated on layers of independently known relative order; new results on
three long-standing questions.

## 2. Results (≈2,800 w, figure-driven)
2.1 **One ordering from two lenses.** Corpus + the two-lens design in one
paragraph each (sandhi and preprocessing to SI); hero maps; convergence.
*Fig 1: map pair + convergence heatmap.*
2.2 **The axis is the signature of correlated drift.** Null-model trio:
no-structure vs heterogeneity vs drift; only drift makes a dominant,
ordering-shaped, length-independent axis. *Fig 2: null-model panel.*
2.3 **Losses are the clock.** Split-half retention decomposition: loss
component alone ρ 0.939; gains 0.72. *Fig 3: loss/gain.*
2.4 **Validation on known relative order.** Critical-edition apparatus
(known-later) measures later, both lenses; source-layer inside host
purāṇas measures earlier than the hosts' shared late text; a genre
control bounds the one real confound. *Fig 4: E1 dumbbell + stratigraphy
strip.*
2.5 **What the clock says.** Three headline findings, one paragraph each,
guardrails intact: (i) the genealogical proto-purāṇa (Kirfel's
pañcalakṣaṇa core) is the oldest purāṇic stratum, matching a chain of
dated external witnesses that speak of "the purāṇa" in the singular;
(ii) the Mahābhārata's closing books pattern with the oldest epic
language — an early-fixed kernel appended late as books; (iii) the
Bhāgavata's position is scale-dependent and sui generis; combined with a
three-channel search finding no datable quotation before ~1000, the
method sharpens — without settling — a famous dating controversy
(symmetric wording, no "archaizer" premise). *Fig 5: annotated map.*

## 3. Discussion (≈1,200 w)
Why retention works (autocorrelated change, community structure in gains);
what it does NOT claim: absolute dates; book age (language age ≠ book age
— transmission preserves or levels); the second map dimension is register,
not a second chronology. Transferability: any long accreting tradition
(other scriptural corpora, legal codes, liturgies). Limits: unit length
floor; editorial mediation; the BhP question left open by design.

## 4. Methods (≈1,500 w main + SI)
Corpus and manifest; ByT5 unsandhi; no-space trigrams; Delta/MDS;
fixed-map projection + line-bootstrap CIs; null-model construction;
split-half retention design; robustness battery (MFW, metric, reuse,
names, jackknife, independent stylo replication — one table); colophon
cleanup. Data/code availability.
